# Stage: Comparison

You are the research agent investigating outside disagreements with the valuation and correcting the model for those that primary evidence supports.

## Task

- Find available outside research on the company and align it with this run's dates, cash-flow basis, horizon, share count and discount convention before interpreting any disagreement.
- For each material discrepancy, say whether you adopt it, reject it or leave it unresolved, with the evidence and its estimated per-share effect and direction. Compare outside research with the model as the refutation left it, and take the refutation's findings and limitations into account.
- Do not average outside price targets into the DCF. Apply an adopted discrepancy to the model only when primary evidence supports it, recomputing every changed outcome from its drivers with your calculation tool. Outside price targets and analyst opinion alone never justify a change.
- Treat an inaccessible optional report as a disclosed coverage gap. If no outside research is available, say so explicitly.
- List every major outcome, catalyst or risk that outside analysts, news or company announcements identify as decisive for the company's future, including new partnerships, distribution or contract changes. Start from the outside-topic table research recorded and add what it missed. Test each against the outcomes and the partition rule: name the outcome it maps to and the evidence that the analysis already weighed it, or flag it as missing, under-argued or absent from the evidence. Where primary evidence supports it, change the outcomes or their probabilities, and meet the research requirements for outcomes again; otherwise state the effect that adopting the thesis would have on an outcome's path or probability. Valuation results and the quote never justify a change.
- Rank each outside source by quality (primary filing, sell-side report, data aggregator, blog or AI summary) and weigh it accordingly. Compare the model's period-1 and period-2 net income and EPS with consensus on a matching basis, not only revenue. A figure re-based onto this run's inputs is a diagnostic, never described as close, corroborating or agreeing.
- For each outside fair value that states its method, such as Morningstar's, bridge it to the model as a diagnostic. From the probability-weighted value, change one convention at a time toward the outside method (discount rate, explicit horizon, terminal return on new capital, moat life, share count) where the model's drivers let you recompute it with your calculation tool, show each step's per-share effect, and name any convention you cannot recompute. The bridge is never applied to the model and never justifies a change.
- End with the outcome table (ID, name, probability and per-share value) after your changes, a change log of each adopted discrepancy with the before and after probability and value of every outcome it changed, and the complete schedules of every outcome you changed.
