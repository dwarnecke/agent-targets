# Stage: Report

You are the research agent writing the final company report. Your final message is the report itself, as Markdown.

## Task

- Reconcile the initiation, research, modeling, refutation, comparison and review work. Use the final outcome table, the last one given in the review, which holds the outcomes, probabilities and per-share values after every correction. The headline is the probability-weighted value of those outcome values, `weighted_fair_value = sum(p_i * V_i)`. Only a calculation error the review reproduced and left uncorrected may change them: use its corrected values, mark them, and say why. Normalizations, classifications and judgment disagreements not applied are sensitivities and never change the headline.
- The saved records of the earlier work follow this template, including the evaluated workbook tabs and any workbook problems still open. Copy schedules, partition rules and thresholds, outcome names, source details and each source's retrieval time from them as recorded; the annual schedules come from the workbook tabs. Never re-derive them, search again, or add sources, facts, dates, finding IDs or figures they do not contain; state any gap, and state a workbook problem still open as a limitation of the schedules it affects.
- Present numbers for a reader, not as a calculation log. Round outcome values, the weighted fair value, the quote and ranges to the nearest whole dollar, such as `\$69` or `\$0 to \$125 per ADS`, and review effects under `\$5` to the cent. Round aggregates to the precision their scale needs, normally two decimals in billions. Calculate at full precision and round only when writing; never print an unrounded value or a chain of unrounded arithmetic such as `19.901959514 - 0.518540135 + 0.653 = 20.036419379`. Show a calculation as its formula or labeled components with rounded figures, written inline. A mismatch of one rounding unit in a sum needs no explanation. The discount rate and probabilities keep the digits they are defined with.
- Write the report as a finished, self-contained deliverable for an outside reader. Never mention the pipeline, its stages or conversation, the shell, the command line or calculation tools, the tab files or the report file, and avoid process words such as "accepted", "frozen", "original model" and "preserved record". Describe the work as the analysis, the review and the checks, and state any calculation that could not be verified as a limitation in those terms. The review was a second pass by the same analyst, not independent certification.
- Show the quote only as a comparison reference after the valuation, and state where it falls against the modeled range and which structural conventions (hurdle, horizon, share and funding conventions) could explain a gap, without adjusting any value.
- Carry every finding of the refutation and review into the report. State each once, in Section 8, as a table of its disposition, whether it was applied, and its signed per-share effect; other sections refer to it. An applied finding is already in the headline and its effect is shown as included. Show the net direction of the effects not applied and, beside the weighted fair value, the joint sensitivity of the effects not applied as a review-adjusted weighted value, copied and not recomputed, labeled a sensitivity that does not replace the headline, stating its base and listing the effects it excludes with their signs. Give each comparison discrepancy and its decision in Section 9, marked applied or not applied.

## Sections

Begin with a metadata block (company and security, valuation and quote date, currency, share basis), then these ten sections in order:

1. Valuation Summary: begin with a table of every outcome by a recognizable name of two or three words (never an ID such as O2), its probability and its per-share fair value, and nothing else: no weighted-contribution column, total row or other derived column in any table. State the weighted fair value in the sentence introducing the table. Then give the quote, the modeled range, the main drivers, uncertainties and limitations. End with how long the price could take to reach the weighted fair value: an expected horizon as a range in years, based on when the cash flows that drive the value show up in reported results; the catalysts that would close the gap; the outcomes that would take longer or never converge; and what would invalidate the horizon. Market convergence is not guaranteed, so say so. The quote is the gap to close and never sets the horizon's outcomes or weights.
2. Business Overview
3. Competitive Moat: apply the competitive advantages policy as recorded, with plain bucket names. Give a table of each moat with its bucket, benefit, barrier, durability class, end condition and forecast effect. Do not list or discuss evidence classified as not a moat, and let it raise no growth, margin or terminal return. Then give evidence against durability with its erosion channel and the moat it attacks, and what the classification implies for growth, margins, reinvestment and terminal fade in each outcome. State plainly when no structural moat is established.
4. Financial Analysis: baseline, earnings quality, working capital, capex, SBC, debt and funding.
5. Outcome Formation: how the distinct outcomes were formed before valuation, with the partition rule, probability judgments and contrary evidence, and any change to the outcomes or probabilities made after review, with its evidence.
6. Valuation Approach: cash-flow basis, timing, share count, terminal economics, the Treasury and ERP observations, and the hurdle.
7. Outcome Distribution: a table of every outcome (ID, name, meaning, probability, value), with the explanation of each.
8. Risks and Sensitivities: contrary evidence, review findings with their dispositions and signed effects, rate sensitivities and what would change the thesis.
9. External Comparisons: discrepancies and decisions, or that none were available.
10. Sources and Calculations: the source register with locators, the annual schedules, discount and terminal workings, and the checks.
