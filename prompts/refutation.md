# Stage: Refutation

You are a reviewer challenging the completed research and financial model in this conversation, and then correcting it. Treat your earlier work as claims to test, not as facts: re-derive and re-verify rather than confirming what you remember. Find consequential errors and weak assumptions. Do not invent objections; if the analysis holds up, say so and change nothing.

## Task

- Review the research and modeling you produced, looking especially for shortcuts, assumptions chosen without evidence, and checks skipped or failed. Verify material source claims against the original sources, and research further wherever a claim, assumption or apparent contradiction needs testing.
- Challenge the outcomes: omitted alternatives, overlap, dependencies and probability rationales. Judge exclusivity and coverage against the research partition rule. Probabilities are subjective by design, so object only when the rationale is missing, contradicts the evidence, or rests on an overlapping or incomplete partition.
- Test the competitive advantage classification: reclassify any item whose barrier is not in place, check each durability class against expiry dates, rival entry and demand depletion, look for omitted erosion channels, and confirm no item that is not a moat raised growth, margins or terminal returns. A misclassification is an assumption finding with a signed per-share effect.
- Attack the economics: growth against reinvestment, margin durability, competitive response, working capital, SBC, debt and ownership claims, funding of negative cash flow, excess assets and terminal fade.
- Independently recompute every outcome's trailing-twelve-month baseline, bridge, timing, discounting, terminal value and per-share value with your calculation tool, and check the discount rate and the probability total. Derive each recomputation from the stated drivers and reported history without reprinting the model's schedule, and test period-1 revenue, margin, capex and D&A against current guidance and the latest reported run rate. Apply the partition rule to each modeled schedule, test each fade by holding its ratios at the pre-fade year, and measure return on new capital on NOPAT. Show your workings and your own value for each outcome beside the model's.
- Report the findings first, then correct the model. Apply every calculation error you reproduced and every material finding the evidence supports, so the outcomes, probabilities and valuations in this conversation end up corrected. Minor findings and those the evidence does not support stay unapplied as sensitivities. Change an outcome or probability only for the defects named above or a sourced finding. Valuation results, the quote and outside price targets never justify a change. A revised set of outcomes meets the research requirements for outcomes again. Recompute every changed outcome from its drivers with your calculation tool and not by patching the old schedule, and build a new or redefined outcome's forward DCF as modeling did.
- List each finding with its severity (material or minor), affected outcomes, evidence, explanation and the correction you recommend, and give each material assumption a disposition: supported, weakened, rejected or unresolved. Label each finding a calculation error (an arithmetic, transcription or formula mistake you reproduced) or an assumption, and give calculation errors corrected per-outcome and weighted values to the cent. Before calling a contradiction between stages a model error, say which side is wrong.
- Give each material finding a signed per-share effect on every affected outcome and on the weighted value, tagged an estimate or a bound, or say why it cannot be computed, and mark it applied or not applied. Give one joint sensitivity of the effects not applied only, flagging overlaps. Look for upward and downward biases alike. A material finding is a plain statement of what is wrong and its effect; it does not stop the pipeline.
- End with the corrected outcome table (ID, name, probability and per-share value), a change log of each applied finding with the before and after probability and value of every outcome it changed, and the complete schedules of every outcome you changed.
